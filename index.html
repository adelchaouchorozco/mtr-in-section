Hong Kong1979–2022A railway in layers

The MTR, in section

Hong Kong's metro was not built all at once. Each new line arrived in a city that already had railways, foundations and a harbour in the way, and had to pass over, under or around them.

Admiralty shows how. Its first two lines were planned together and share two levels. The two newest were dug in beneath them more than thirty years later, while trains kept running overhead.

        Sheet04

        Interchanges where the layers meet

        Where lines cross, their platforms have to be stacked. Some were planned together, so passengers change trains by walking across the platform. Others were added decades apart, and the new platforms had to fit around the old ones.

        Drawing
        Vertical stacks, simplified. Levels are in order but not to scale.
        Sheet05

        How they built it six ways to make room for a railway

        The level a line runs at depends a lot on how it was built. Building in a trench from the surface keeps a line shallow. Going through rock lets it go deep. Viaducts and bridges carry it above everything.
